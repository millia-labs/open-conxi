# SOP 09: Stayover service

Owner: executive housekeeper, and the floor supervisor on shift.
Used by: turnover-board.
When: a guest is staying on and the room is on your board as a stayover.

## Standard
- A stayover is 0.67 credit, about 20 minutes.
- Guest belongings are tidied in place. Never moved to another surface, never opened, never read.
- Do Not Disturb is respected. If a room has had no entry for 24 hours, a manager does a welfare check with a second person.

## Steps
READ-DO.
1. Check the door first. Do Not Disturb sign or light on: do not knock. Note the time on your sheet and come back after [your hotel: second pass time].
2. Knock three times, say "Housekeeping", wait. Guest inside: ask whether they want service now or later, and write down the answer.
3. Empty bins and ashtrays, collect used glasses and cups. Leave anything that could be the guest's: papers, bags, medicine, food, even if it looks like rubbish.
4. Bathroom top to bottom. Clean and dry mirror, shower, basin and toilet. Replace towels left on the floor or in the bath; towels hung up stay unless [your hotel: linen reuse policy] says otherwise.
5. Make the bed. Change linen on [your hotel: linen change interval] or when soiled. Clothes on the bed are folded and left on the bed.
6. Dust high to low round the guest's things. Laptops, phones, jewellery and cash are not touched. Wipe handles, switches and remote.
7. Restock amenities, tea tray and water to par. Post minibar consumption.
8. Floor last, working back to the door. Close curtains and set aircon to [your hotel: stayover setting] only if the guest has not set their own.

## Pause point
DO-CONFIRM, before you leave the room.
- [ ] No guest belonging moved, opened or thrown away.
- [ ] Safe, laptop and cash left exactly as found.
- [ ] Door closed and locked behind you; check it with a push.
- [ ] Any Do Not Disturb or refused service written on your sheet with the time.

## Escalate to the GM when
A room has had no entry for 24 hours (Do Not Disturb, refused service, or no answer): the duty manager goes with a second person, knocks, announces, and enters only to check the guest is safe. Also escalate anything that suggests a guest is unwell, in danger, or putting others in danger.

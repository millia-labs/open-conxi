# SOP 11: Lost and found

Owner: executive housekeeper, and the housekeeping supervisor on shift.
Used by: turnover-board.
When: anyone finds an item a guest or visitor has left behind, in a room or a public area.

## Standard
- Logged the same shift it is found, bagged and tagged.
- Valuables go in the safe with two signatures.
- The owner's identity is confirmed before anything is released.
- Never tell a caller what was found in a room until you have verified they are the registered guest.

## Steps
READ-DO.
1. Do not open bags, wallets or phones beyond what you need to identify the owner. Take the item to the housekeeping office before the end of your shift.
2. Log it: date, time, room or place found, finder's name, and a plain description (colour, make, contents seen).
3. Bag it, seal it, and tag it with the log number. Food and opened toiletries are thrown away and logged as such.
4. Valuables (cash, cards, passports, jewellery, phones, laptops) go in the safe. The finder and the supervisor both sign the log and the tag.
5. Guest calls or writes: ask them to describe the item first. Check they are the registered guest for that room and date before you confirm anything was found.
6. Release in person: check photo ID against the booking and the description. The collector signs the log; valuables are handed over with two staff signatures.
7. Posting it: guest pays or agrees the cost first, address confirmed in writing from the email on the booking, tracking number logged.
8. Unclaimed after [your hotel: retention period]: dispose of it per [your hotel: disposal rule], and log how and who.

## Pause point
DO-CONFIRM, before any item leaves the office.
- [ ] Log number on the tag matches the log.
- [ ] Owner confirmed as the registered guest, or holds the guest's written authority.
- [ ] Photo ID checked, collector has signed.
- [ ] Valuables released with two staff signatures.

## Escalate to the GM when
You find cash over [your hotel: cash threshold], a passport, weapons, drugs or anything that looks like evidence; police ask about an item; or a guest says something was taken from the room rather than lost.
